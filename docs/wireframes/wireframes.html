Family Wishlist – wireframes v1

Milestone 1 of SPEC.md v0.3 · 2026-11-05 · for review by Kevin

These are low-fidelity screens for the guest flow (home → person → item sheet → one-tap "Mark purchased") and the owner admin. Please review the structure and interaction: what's on each screen, what one tap does, and whether anything from the spec is missing or snuck in that shouldn't be there (there is deliberately no name entry, no sign-in, and no "planning to buy" step). Grey boxes stand in for product photos. Colors and type are placeholders everywhere except section 5, where two visual directions for the item card are shown so you can pick one. Section 6 lists questions that came up while drawing.

  1. Filter chips scroll sideways. "Available only" is a toggle and defaults on if the season banner is set, so a guest in December sees what's left. Category comes from `@labels`; price band is bucketed from `~$`; occasion from the `occasion:` hint.
  2. Sort defaults to desire (p1 → p3 → unprioritized), with price and newest as the other two. Count line reflects filters.
  3. Item card: placeholder photo (R2-cached `og:image`, grey box on failure), name, desire badge from Todoist priority, owner's about-price as "~$45", category tag, and the best offer from the 3-offer panel. Tapping the card opens the item sheet (screen 3).
  4. Quantity items (`qty: 2`) show progress in the status pill rather than hiding at the first purchase; the card stays fully opaque until all N are taken.
  5. Purchased card stays in the list, dimmed and struck through, so a guest who bought it still sees their mark and the owner sees it mirrored from Todoist. It disappears only when "Available only" is on.
  6. No badge, no "~$" when the owner typed only a task name (p4 default, no `price:` hint) – the card degrades gracefully instead of showing empty slots; the best found price fills in when the price routine runs.

Saved. Todoist gets the @bought label in the background; if that fails it retries – you're done either way.
  1. Bottom sheet slides over the person list; the dimmed list stays behind it so the guest keeps their place. Drag down or tap the scrim to close. Without JS the same content renders as a full page at `/robbie/item-1842`.
  2. Notes are the Todoist task description verbatim (minus the parsed `price:`/`qty:`/`occasion:` hints and the first URL, which becomes the product link). Size and color live here; the owner never fills a form.
  3. Where to buy lists at most 3 offers, cheapest highlighted as "Best". Each shows store, price, "checked N days ago" from `offers.fetched_at`, and its source. Stale offers stay – nothing expires, and guests never trigger a lookup.
  4. One big button. Tapping it writes a `purchases` row and flips the UI immediately. There is no name field, no confirm, no "planning to buy" – one tap is the whole flow.
  5. Status pill flips to "Purchased" in the sheet and on the card behind it (now dimmed). For `qty: N` items it reads "1 of 2 purchased" and the button stays active for the next guest.
  6. "Purchased ✓" with Undo. The device that marked it can undo silently (browser token). Any other device tapping Undo gets one confirm – "Did someone buy this by mistake?" – then it's released and Todoist gets the `@bought` label removed plus a comment.

Everything about the items themselves (name, notes, price hint, who it's for) is edited in Todoist, not here.
  1. Cloudflare Access sits in front of `/admin`; the app has zero auth code. Only kkutner@icloud.com is allow-listed. Guests who stumble on `/admin` see Cloudflare's login page, nothing of ours.
  2. Actions: Sync now (Todoist → site), Refresh prices (re-run page fetch and queue the Claude price routine), Rotate guest link (new slug; old link dies immediately – a confirm warns about re-sending it), Archive season (confirm), Export JSON (one file, same shape as the nightly R2 export).
  3. Counts per person mirror the home page plus the owner-only numbers (hidden items, partial qty).
  4. Purchased list is the registry view: what's been taken, when, and whether the `@bought` write reached Todoist. There is no column for who – guests are anonymous by design.
  5. Health is `/admin/health` surfaced inline: last sync, last price refresh, queued outbox writes with retry timer, and errors. The same data drives the optional "sync failed for 24 h" email.
  6. Season banner is the only free-text setting on the site. Changing it never touches items.

6 · Open questions for the owner

Things the spec leaves open that I had to pick a side on to draw. Each shows what the wireframes assume; say the word and it flips.

  1. Purchased items: sink to the bottom or stay in place? Drawn as "stay in place, dimmed" (screen 2, card 5) so a guest who just bought something still finds it where it was. Sinking them keeps the available items together but makes a just-marked card jump, which can look like it vanished.
  2. Which price is the headline on the card: your "~$45" or the best found "$42"? Drawn with your about-price as the headline and "Best price $42 at Target" as a second line. The alternative is to lead with the real best offer and only fall back to "~$" when no offer exists. Leading with the real price is more useful; leading with yours is more stable (offers go stale).
  3. Should "Available only" default to on? Drawn as on whenever a season banner is set, off otherwise. If it's always on, a guest who already bought something can't see their own mark without flipping a chip; if it's always off, December lists get cluttered with struck-through cards.
  4. qty: 2 items after the first purchase – what does the button say? Drawn as the same "Mark purchased" button with the pill reading "1 of 2 purchased". Alternatives: "Mark another purchased", or a small "1 of 2" stepper. Also: should Undo on a qty item release the most recent mark only (drawn) or ask which?
  5. Home page: should the cards show anything beyond name and counts? Drawn minimal (initial, name, counts). Could add a "Really want" teaser (first p1 item) or a tiny row of thumbnails, but both make the home page compete with the person page. Also: initials vs. real photos vs. nothing for the avatar.
